A Guide for Restaurant Owners

Everything that happens between an order and a closed drawer, in one place.

Restro is the system running the front counter, the kitchen, the delivery riders, and the books — all at once, for one restaurant or a whole group of them. This page walks through what it actually does, in plain terms.

1

Taking an order

The counter screen is where every sale starts — dine-in, takeaway, or delivery — and it's built to be fast enough for a lunchtime rush.

One screen, any order type

A cashier picks products, adjusts quantities, and attaches a customer — the same flow whether it's a table, a pickup, or a courier run.

Kitchen sees it instantly

Once an order is sent, it appears on the kitchen display as a ticket — no paper slips changing hands, no shouting orders across the pass.

Receipts print automatically

Every order that's charged, printed for later, or handed to a courier gets its own receipt — in Arabic or English, matched to how it was actually paid.

Customers are remembered

A returning customer's name, phone, and address are already on file — no re-typing an address for the fifth delivery this month.

2

Getting paid — four honest ways

Not every order gets paid at the counter. A delivery rider might collect cash hours later; a regular customer might run a tab until the end of the day. Restro tracks each path differently, on purpose.

Paid now

Cash or card, on the spot

Receipt prints, the sale lands in the drawer immediately. Nothing left to chase.

Pay at end of day

Collected from the rider later

Receipt prints now; the money is counted once the delivery agent settles up.

Print only, not paid

A running tab

The receipt goes out with the order, but payment is still owed — tracked the same way as an end-of-day delivery.

Cancelled

As if it never happened

No receipt, no charge, nothing added to the drawer. A clean undo.

Anything still owed shows up on the Pending Payments screen — searchable by delivery rider, customer, order number, or date — until someone marks it collected.

3

One order's full journey

Here's what actually happens behind a single delivery order placed on credit.

1

Cashier builds the order

Picks the items, attaches the customer, assigns a delivery rider, and marks it "pay at end of day."

2

Receipt prints, rider leaves

The order is now waiting — it doesn't touch the cash drawer yet.

3

It appears on Pending Payments

Filed under that rider's name, with the order total and how long it's been waiting.

4

Rider returns with the cash

A cashier finds the order (by rider, by name, or by order number) and taps Collect.

5

Drawer updates, instantly

The cash total goes up by exactly that order's amount, the order disappears from the pending list, and no second receipt gets printed.

4

Always knowing what's in the drawer

Cash on hand isn't something anyone eyeballs at close — it's a running total, built from every sale and every collection as they happen.

One number, everywhere

The counter, the manager's dashboard, and the pending-payments screen all read the same live figure — settle a payment on one screen and the others update without anyone refreshing.

A record for every move

Every peso or pound that enters or leaves the drawer — a sale, a collection, a cash advance, a cancelled order's reversal — leaves a paper trail behind it.

5

The dashboard

The first screen an owner sees — a real-time read on how the restaurant is doing, not a pile of raw numbers to interpret.

Today, at a glance

Sales, order counts, and cash-in-drawer for the day, updating as orders come in rather than at end-of-shift.

Trends, not just totals

Revenue and order charts over time, so a slow Tuesday or a busy weekend is visible, not just felt.

6

Menu & products

Every item sold at the counter starts here — its price, its cost to make, and what that means for margin.

Price and cost, side by side

Set what a dish sells for and what it costs to make, so profit per item is visible, not guessed at.

Change it once, everywhere

Update a price or take an item off the menu, and every till reflects it on the next order — no reprinting a paper menu.

7

Customers, loyalty & complaints

The people who keep coming back are worth remembering — and the ones who had a bad experience are worth following up on.

A customer list, not a notebook

Name, phone, and delivery address saved once and reused on every future order — searchable by phone number at the counter.

Loyalty points, earned automatically

Regulars build up points on every purchase with no extra step for the cashier.

Complaints, tracked to resolution

A complaint gets logged against the customer and order it belongs to, so it doesn't just disappear after the conversation ends.

8

Stock & waste

Knowing what's in the kitchen before you run out of it — and knowing where it went if it didn't get sold.

Stock levels & low-stock alerts

Ingredient quantities update as they're used, with a warning before something runs out mid-service.

Goods checks & waste logging

Damaged, expired, or missing stock gets logged with a reason and a photo — so shrinkage has an explanation attached to it, not a shrug.

9

The team

Everything tied to a member of staff lives on their one record — from the moment they clock in to the day they're paid.

Time clock & attendance

Staff clock in and out; lateness and overtime are tracked automatically and can be excused or approved by a manager.

Scheduling

Build the week's shifts once, and every worker sees where they're expected to be.

Salaries & cash advances

Pay is calculated from attendance and overtime, and any cash advanced against a future paycheck is tracked and deducted automatically.

Money beyond the drawer

Petty cash

Small day-to-day expenses logged as they happen, instead of a shoebox of receipts at month-end.

Rent & recurring costs

Fixed monthly costs tracked alongside daily sales, so profit isn't calculated on revenue alone.

Full profit & loss

Income, expenses, and cash flow rolled into one finance view.

Exportable reports

Sales, staff, and P&L reports as PDF or Excel — ready to hand to an accountant or a bank.

11

Reservations

Bookings and a waitlist in the same system as everything else, so the host stand isn't a notebook that only one person can read.

12

Multiple branches, kept in sync

One system can run a single restaurant or a whole group of them, without their data ever mixing.

Fully separate data

Each restaurant's orders, customers, staff, and cash figures are walled off from every other one on the platform — nothing crosses over.

Settings per restaurant

Tax rates, printer setup, and business details are configured once per location and stay local to it.

A visible sync status

A dedicated screen shows whether a device is fully synced with the cloud or still catching up — no wondering if a change went through.

Who can do what

Three levels of access, so a cashier can run a full shift without needing an owner in the building — and an owner can see everything a cashier can't touch.

Cashier

Runs the floor

Takes orders, manages customers, collects pending payments, sees the drawer total.

Admin / Owner

Runs the restaurant

Everything a cashier can do, plus staff, reports, pricing, and settings.

Super Admin

Runs the platform

Creates restaurants, issues their licenses, and manages accounts across every location — reserved for the operator running the software itself.

Works offline. A lost internet connection doesn't stop a shift — the app keeps running locally and catches up once it's back. Arabic & English. Every screen, including the layout direction, switches fully between the two.

Getting started

Setting up a new restaurant takes minutes, not a technician visit.

The platform operator creates your restaurant

They set up your restaurant's account and hand you a license — this fixes how many devices you can use and how many days you can run offline before reconnecting.

The owner activates it

The first sign-in asks for a one-time activation code. After that, it's just a username and password from any registered device.

Add products, staff, and delivery riders

Set up the menu, add cashiers and delivery agents, and the counter is ready to take its first order.

Cashiers sign in and start selling

No activation step for staff — they just log in with the username and password the owner gave them.

15

Common questions

What happens if the internet drops mid-shift?

Nothing stops. Orders, payments, and everything else keep working against the device's own local copy of the data. Once the connection returns, it quietly syncs back up — nobody has to redo anything.

Can two members of staff share the same name?

Yes. Every delivery rider and every staff account is tracked by an internal ID, not by their display name — so two riders both named "Ahmed" are never confused with each other when settling payments.

Can I use it on more than one till or device?

Yes, up to the number of devices set on your license. Every device signs in independently, and everything they do shows up on every other screen immediately.

Is one restaurant's data ever visible to another?

No. Every restaurant on the platform is completely walled off from every other one — orders, customers, staff, and cash figures never cross between them.

What happens when a license expires?

The restaurant is notified before it happens. Once expired, staff are blocked from signing in until the owner renews it — no data is lost while that's sorted out.

Do I need to print a second receipt when a pending payment is finally collected?

No — the receipt already went out when the order was placed. Collecting the payment later just settles the money; it doesn't print anything new.
